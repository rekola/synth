# Chords of the otonal and utonal scales

The otonal scale (1/1 7/6 5/4 4/3 3/2 5/3 7/4) is harmonics 12, 14, 15, 16,
18, 20 and 21 of the note a fifth below its tonic, so any notes taken from it
are a slice of one harmonic series: every chord in it is a set of harmonics of
one fundamental, though a few (12:14:21, 21:24:28) are simpler read as
undertones. The utonal scale (1/1 8/7 6/5 4/3 3/2 8/5 12/7) is its mirror,
undertones of the note a twelfth above its tonic, and the converse holds for
it. The mirror turns each otonal chord a:b:c into the utonal 1/c:1/b:1/a,
upside down: a major triad becomes a minor one, a subminor triad a supermajor
one. Both scales are shown here on C: otonal C D♯ E F G A A♯ (harmonics of the
F below) and utonal C E𝄫 E♭ F G A♭ B𝄫 (undertones of the G above).

Chords are listed in root position: the notes stacked upward from the lowest
within one octave. Intervals are between neighbouring notes, bottom to top. A
proportion written with fractions is the same chord as the whole numbers after
it.

## Triads

A triad divides a consonant interval in two. The familiar ones divide the
fifth (3:2) into two thirds, but dividing the fourth (4:3) into a third and a
second is no less a choice. Here a triad that spans a fourth is called a narrow
triad. The two divisions match: 4:5:6 divides the fifth at its arithmetic mean
and 10:12:15 at its harmonic mean, and 6:7:8 and 21:24:28 do the same for the
fourth. The fifth divides into two thirds in four ways in these scales, all
listed; of the fourth only the two divisions at its means are listed, and its
other divisions, into a 5-limit third and a second, are left out.

| Span | Chord | Proportion | Intervals | Otonal | Utonal |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fifth | Major | 4:5:6 | 5:4, 6:5 | C E G, F A C | A♭ C E♭ |
| Fifth | Minor | 10:12:15 | 6:5, 5:4 | A C E | C E♭ G, F A♭ C |
| Fifth | Subminor | 6:7:9 | 7:6, 9:7 | C D♯ G | E𝄫 F B𝄫 |
| Fifth | Supermajor | 14:18:21 | 9:7, 7:6 | D♯ G A♯ | F B𝄫 C |
| Fourth | Narrow major | 6:7:8 | 7:6, 8:7 | C D♯ F, G A♯ C | B𝄫 C E𝄫 |
| Fourth | Narrow minor | 1/8:1/7:1/6 (21:24:28) | 8:7, 7:6 | A♯ C D♯ | C E𝄫 F, G B𝄫 C |

Every degree of both scales but one is the root of a triad of one span or the
other. Otonal G and A♯ and utonal G and B𝄫 have no fifth above them and carry
only narrow triads. A♯'s is 21:24:28; above it the scale has no third, and F is
27 cents wider than a fifth. Otonal D♯, F and A and utonal E𝄫, F and A♭ have
no fourth above them and carry only fifth-span triads. Otonal E and utonal E♭
have no fifth above them, and their fourth divides only into a 5-limit third
and a second (15:18:20, 12:15:16 and their mirrors), so they carry no triad
listed here.

| C | C E G, C D♯ G | C D♯ F | | C | C E♭ G | C E𝄫 F |
| D♯ | D♯ G A♯ | | | E𝄫 | E𝄫 F B𝄫 | |
| E | | | | E♭ | | |
| F | F A C | | | F | F A♭ C, F B𝄫 C | |
| G | | G A♯ C | | G | | G B𝄫 C |

The other three-note chords are inversions of these, or sets with wider
proportions. G A♯ D♯, for example, is 18:21:28: the supermajor triad on D♯
with G in the bass.

## Tuning

Played live, recorded or typed, and after `apply-just-intonation-region`, each
note of either scale sounds at its exact ratio to the key, so these chords
come out pure ([user-interface.md](user-interface.md#just-intonation)).
Without a tuning correction, 31 and 53-EDO come within 6 cents of every ratio;
12 and 19-EDO only approximate the septimal ones.
